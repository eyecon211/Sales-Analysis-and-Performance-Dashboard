# Sales-Analysis-and-Performance-Dashboard

## Project Overview

This Sales Analytics project focuses on analyzing sales data to understand business performance, customer purchasing behavior, product performance, employee performance, regional sales distribution, and overall revenue trends.


## Business Problem

The organization has a large volume of sales data but needs a clear way to monitor and evaluate its sales performance.

Raw transactional data makes it difficult for management to quickly identify:

High-performing and low-performing products
Strong and weak sales regions
Top-performing employees
Sales trends over time
Customers contributing significantly to revenue
Employees meeting or missing their sales targets
Areas requiring management attention
Opportunities for improving revenue and sales performance

Therefore, this project aims to transform the raw sales data into an interactive analytical solution that provides management with a clear overview of business performance.


## Project Objectives

The main objective of this project is to analyze sales data and develop a dashboard that provides actionable insights into business performance.

Analyze total sales and revenue generated.
Identify the highest-performing products.
Analyze sales performance by region.
Evaluate employee sales performance.
Compare actual sales against sales targets.
Calculate employee performance percentages.
Identify top-performing and underperforming employees.
Analyze monthly and yearly sales trends.
Identify the highest-value customers.
Analyze product quantity and sales contribution.
Identify areas with declining or low sales performance.
Develop an interactive dashboard for management decision-making.
Provide recommendations based on the findings.

## Data Cleaning and Preparation


Before conducting the analysis, the raw dataset was inspected and prepared to ensure that the information used in the dashboard was reliable.

The following data-cleaning activities were performed:

1. Removed Duplicate Records

Duplicate transactions were identified and removed where necessary to prevent sales figures from being overstated.

2. Handled Missing Values

Missing values were investigated to determine whether they should be removed, replaced, or retained depending on their business meaning.

3. Standardized Text Fields

Inconsistent spelling and formatting in fields such as:

Employee names
Product names
Regions
Categories

were standardized.

4. Corrected Data Types

Columns were assigned appropriate data types.

Examples include:

Order Date → Date
Quantity → Whole Number
Unit Price → Decimal
Sales → Decimal/Currency
Employee → Text
Region → Text
5. Created Calculated Columns

Additional fields were created where required to support the analysis.

Examples include:

Total Sales
Profit
Sales Variance
Achievement %
Sales Status
Month
Year
Month Name
Performance Ranking

## Key Insights

The analysis provides management with insights into the major drivers of sales performance.

Examples of insights that can be derived from the dashboard include:

A small number of products may contribute a significant proportion of total revenue.
Sales performance can vary considerably between regions.
Employee performance may differ significantly when actual sales are compared with targets.
Certain periods may experience higher sales than others.
High-volume products do not necessarily generate the highest revenue.
Some customers may contribute disproportionately to total sales.
Regions with lower sales may represent opportunities for targeted sales strategies.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analytical findings, the following recommendations can be considered:

1. Improve Employee Performance Monitoring

Management should regularly monitor employee sales against targets to identify performance gaps early.

2. Recognize High-Performing Employees

Employees consistently exceeding their targets can be recognized through appropriate incentive and recognition programs.

3. Support Underperforming Employees

Employees consistently below target should be assessed to determine whether they require:

Additional training
Better sales support
Improved customer allocation
Clearer targets
Additional resources
4. Focus on High-Performing Products

Products generating significant revenue should receive appropriate attention in inventory planning, marketing, and sales strategies.

5. Investigate Low-Performing Products

Products with consistently low sales should be reviewed to understand whether the issue relates to:

Pricing
Customer demand
Marketing
Product availability
Competition
6. Strengthen Regional Strategies

Regions with strong performance should be studied to understand the factors contributing to their success.

Regions with weaker performance should be investigated for possible market and operational challenges.

7. Use Data for Target Setting

Future sales targets should be informed by historical sales trends, regional performance, product demand, and employee performance rather than relying solely on fixed assumptions.

8. Monitor Sales Trends Regularly

Management should use the dashboard as an ongoing monitoring tool rather than relying only on periodic reports.
